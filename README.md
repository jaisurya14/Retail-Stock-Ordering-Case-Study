# Stock Ordering Process Improvement: Business Analysis Case Study

A business analysis of the stock ordering process at an IGA supermarket in Melbourne, with a recommended improvement and a pilot plan.

📄 **[Read the full case study (PDF)](Stock-Ordering-BA-Case-Study.pdf)**

## The problem

Ordering is reactive. Items are reordered only once both the shelf and the top capping are empty, and quantities follow Metcash deals rather than actual sales. This takes about 6 staff-hours a week (estimate). It causes specials to sell out before the next delivery, while other lines, especially bread, are over-ordered and expire.

## What I did

- **Stakeholder analysis:** informal interviews with team members, plus my own observation as a team member (stock filling and register)
- **As-is process map:** swimlane diagram (Manager, Metcash, Floor staff) with 5 pain points identified
- **To-be process map:** a redesigned flow with a rotating stock lead and orders drafted from sales data
- **Requirements:** 10 user stories with acceptance criteria, prioritised with MoSCoW, plus non-functional requirements
- **Options analysis:** 4 options compared on cost, benefit and risk (do nothing, dedicated stock controller, data-informed ordering, inventory software)
- **KPIs:** 4 measures with baselines and targets for an 8-week pilot

## Recommendation

Data-informed ordering using tools the store already has:

- par levels built from POS sales history
- the Metcash promotions calendar to size special orders
- labelled back-storage locations
- a customer out-of-stock log
- a rotating stock lead on each order day

It needs no new software, and its cost is low.

## Skills demonstrated

Requirements gathering · Stakeholder analysis · Process mapping (as-is / to-be) · User stories and acceptance criteria · MoSCoW prioritisation · Options analysis and business case · KPI design

## Limitations

- Time figures are staff estimates.
- No sales data was used, so the targets are proposals to be confirmed after measuring baselines.
- The store manager has not yet been interviewed.
- No staff names, sales figures or supplier prices are included.

## What I'd do next

- Interview the store manager.
- Measure KPI baselines for 2 weeks.
- Build the par-level spreadsheet from a POS export, and track pilot KPIs in a Power BI dashboard.
